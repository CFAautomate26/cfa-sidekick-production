# Situation Playbooks

Step-by-step handling for the situations that recur in QSR operations. Each
playbook assumes the facts have been gathered per the intake list in
[README.md](./README.md). Where a step says **STOP**, that means get a
risk read (and possibly licensed counsel per the
[escalation matrix](./escalation-matrix.md)) before acting.

---

## 1. Progressive discipline

**Goal**: correct behaviour, and if it doesn't correct, build a record that
supports a clean exit.

1. **Verbal coaching** — still documented: date, issue, expectation, in a
   leader log or HR file. Not given to the employee, but written the same day.
2. **Written warning** — use the template. States the facts (dates,
   specifics), the standard breached (handbook/policy section), the expected
   change, and the consequence line ("further incidents may result in further
   discipline up to and including termination of employment").
3. **Final written warning** — same structure, explicitly labelled final.
4. **Termination** — see playbook 2.

Rules that keep the record clean:

- Discipline the *conduct*, never the person. No character commentary.
- Same-day documentation. A warning written three weeks later reads as
  pretext.
- Consistency is the whole game: if two team members no-show and only one is
  written up, the write-up is worth little and may ground a discrimination
  complaint.
- **Check for protected context before every write-up**: is the absence
  ESA-protected leave? Is the performance dip connected to a disclosed or
  suspected disability or family status issue? Has this employee recently
  raised a complaint (harassment, safety, wages)? If yes to any — **STOP**,
  reprisal/discrimination risk assessment first.
- Have the employee sign as *received* (not agreed). Refusal to sign: note
  "refused to sign, copy provided" with a witness initial.

## 2. Termination — without cause (the default)

Almost every QSR termination should be structured **without cause**. Cause is
a high bar in Ontario and failed cause claims are expensive.

1. **Pull the file**: hire date, age, role, wage, signed employment agreement
   (does it have a termination clause?), discipline history, any protected
   circumstances (pregnancy, disability, recent complaint, leave). Any
   protected circumstance → **STOP**, this becomes a high-risk exit.
2. **Do the math** — two numbers:
   - ESA floor: notice/pay-in-lieu per service band + vacation pay + benefit
     continuation (+ severance pay if 5+ years and $2.5M payroll).
   - Common law exposure if no enforceable clause.
   **Waksdale note**: since *Waksdale v Swegon North America* (ONCA 2020), if
   the "with cause" part of a termination clause offends the ESA (most
   pre-2020 clauses do), the **entire** termination provision is void and
   common law notice applies — even for a without-cause exit. Assume older
   agreements are broken until reviewed.
3. **Decide the package**: ESA-only if the clause is enforceable and the risk
   profile is low; otherwise ESA plus a negotiated amount in exchange for a
   **full and final release**. Never pay above-ESA money without a signed
   release. ESA minimums are owed unconditionally — never make *those*
   conditional on signing anything.
4. **The meeting**: short, two leaders present, scripted. Decision is final —
   no debating the merits. Letter handed over. Final pay within 7 days or the
   next regular pay day, whichever is later. ROE within 5 calendar days
   (code E for dismissal; be accurate — ROE codes get scrutinized).
5. **Logistics**: uniform/property return, system access, GroupMe removal
   same day, team communication that respects privacy ("X is no longer with
   the store; please direct questions to me").

## 3. Termination — for cause

Reserve for the genuinely serious: theft, violence, harassment after warning,
serious safety violations, fraud (time theft with proof). Even then:

1. **Investigate first, terminate second** — suspension (paid, to stay safe)
   pending investigation, not instant dismissal.
2. Proportionality: the misconduct must be incompatible with continued
   employment, viewed against tenure and record (*McKinley v BC Tel*:
   contextual approach; dishonesty alone isn't automatically cause).
3. ESA note: even with common-law cause, ESA notice/severance is only
   eliminated where conduct meets the higher "wilful misconduct" standard —
   sometimes you owe ESA minimums even when you have cause at common law.
4. **STOP — every for-cause termination goes through counsel before the
   meeting.** A failed cause allegation adds bad-faith damages risk.

## 4. Resignations

- Accept in writing, confirm last day, and don't talk anyone out the door
  faster: making a resigning employee leave earlier than their offered notice
  date can convert the resignation into a termination for the gap.
- Heat-of-the-moment quits ("I'm done!" mid-shift) are not resignations until
  confirmed once tempers cool — follow up in writing the next day. Treating a
  blow-up as a quit is a classic constructive-dismissal fact pattern.
- Job abandonment: 2–3 no-shows with documented contact attempts (call, text,
  email at each step) before treating the job as abandoned; confirm by letter.

## 5. Human rights accommodation (disability, family status, creed)

1. Requests need no magic words — "I can't do closes anymore because of my
   daughter's dialysis schedule" *is* a family status accommodation request.
2. **Procedural duty**: engage, get relevant information (functional
   limitations, not diagnosis — you are entitled to restrictions and
   prognosis, not the medical condition itself), consider options seriously,
   document the process.
3. **Substantive duty**: accommodate to **undue hardship** — a high bar
   (cost, outside funding, health/safety are the only Code factors).
   Scheduling inconvenience is not undue hardship for a store this size.
4. The employee must cooperate (provide info, accept reasonable options — the
   duty is to *reasonable* accommodation, not their preferred one).
5. Document every offer, every response. If the path ends at "cannot
   accommodate" — **STOP**, counsel review before any employment consequence.

## 6. Harassment / violence complaints (OHSA)

1. Every complaint — formal or offhand — triggers the duty to investigate
   "appropriate in the circumstances." No ignoring a complaint because it was
   raised casually or the complainant "doesn't want anything done."
2. Interim measures: schedule separation, not punitive to the complainant.
3. Investigator must be neutral — if the allegation touches the Operator or a
   senior leader, use an external investigator (**STOP** — set that up with
   counsel).
4. Interview complainant, respondent, witnesses; contemporaneous notes;
   findings on a balance of probabilities; written results of the
   investigation and any corrective action to both parties.
5. No reprisal against anyone for a good-faith complaint — reprisal exposure
   under OHSA s. 50 is often bigger than the original complaint.

## 7. Medical absences and WSIB

- Off-work injuries: sick leave (3 ESA days) then long-term illness leave /
  disability accommodation analysis as it extends. Ask for functional
  abilities info, offer modified duties in writing.
- Workplace injuries: first aid log everything; health care or lost time →
  **Form 7 within 3 business days**, offer suitable modified work fast (it
  controls claim costs), maintain contact. Re-employment obligation holds for
  employees with 1+ year service.
- Never terminate someone on medical leave or with an open WSIB claim without
  counsel — **STOP**. (It's possible in narrow cases — frustration of
  contract — but it is never a do-it-yourself move.)

## 8. Ministry of Labour complaint or inspection

1. Be cooperative and factual; ESOs have broad powers. Take notes on
   everything provided and said.
2. Never contact the (current or former) employee about their complaint —
   that conversation is reprisal fuel.
3. Pull the records the officer will want: agreements, time records, pay
   statements, vacation records. Gaps in records get resolved against the
   employer.
4. If an order to pay or contravention finding is proposed — **STOP**,
   counsel. Applications for review have short deadlines (30 days).

## 9. Wage/hour hygiene (avoid the complaint in the first place)

Quarterly self-audit: three-hour rule compliance on early cuts; OT after 44
hours actually being paid (watch dual-role people and salaried leads);
eating-period compliance on short-staffed days; vacation pay on termination
pay; public holiday pay math for part-timers; deduction authorizations on
file; student-vs-general minimum wage flips when a student turns 18 or hours
change; records for the new-hire information requirement (25+ employees).
