# Ontario Employment Standards — Working Reference

Statutory baseline for the store under the *Employment Standards Act, 2000*
(ESA) and related legislation. These are **minimums** — the employment
agreement or store policy can give more, never less. A contract term that
gives less than the ESA is void and the ESA standard applies instead.

Figures verified as of **August 2026**. Minimum wage changes every October 1;
confirm current rates at ontario.ca/minimumwage before quoting them.

## Wages

- **General minimum wage**: $17.60/hr (since Oct 1, 2025; indexed to Ontario
  CPI each Oct 1 — check for the 2026 rate after it is announced in spring).
- **Student minimum wage**: $16.60/hr — applies to students **under 18** who
  work ≤ 28 hours/week during the school year, or work during school
  holidays/summer. A 17-year-old working 30+ hrs/week while school is in
  session must get the general rate.
- **Three-hour rule**: an employee who regularly works more than 3 hours a
  day, reports as scheduled, and is sent home early must be paid the greater
  of 3 hours at their regular rate or actual hours worked. This bites on slow
  nights — cutting someone after 90 minutes still costs 3 hours' pay.
- **Deductions**: no deductions from wages for cash shortages, breakage, or
  dine-and-dash losses. Uniform cost deductions require specific written
  authorization stating the amount; deductions cannot take pay below minimum
  wage.
- **Tips**: if tips are pooled, the employer cannot share in the pool
  (limited exception for sole proprietors/partners who regularly perform the
  same work). Written tip-pooling policy required if pooling.
- **Pay statements**: each pay day, showing period, rate, hours, gross,
  deductions, net.

## Hours of work and overtime

- **Daily/weekly caps**: 8 hours/day (or the established regular workday if
  longer) and 48 hours/week, absent written agreements to exceed.
- **Overtime**: 1.5× after **44 hours** in a work week. Averaging requires a
  written averaging agreement. For a salaried manager, check the managerial
  exemption carefully — it applies only where the work is *supervisory or
  managerial in character* and non-managerial tasks are performed only on an
  irregular or exceptional basis. A "Team Lead" who mostly runs a register is
  not exempt.
- **Eating periods**: 30 minutes (unpaid unless the contract says otherwise)
  such that no one works more than 5 consecutive hours. Can be split into two
  15s by agreement. Other breaks (the common "10s") are policy, not statute —
  but if given, short breaks under 30 minutes are generally paid.
- **Rest rules**: 11 consecutive hours off per day; 8 hours between shifts
  (unless total of the two shifts ≤ 13 hours or written agreement says
  otherwise); 24 consecutive hours off per work week or 48 per two weeks.
  Watch this on close-then-open ("clopen") scheduling.

## Public holidays

Ontario has **9** public holidays: New Year's Day, Family Day, Good Friday,
Victoria Day, Canada Day, Labour Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas Day, Boxing Day.
(Civic Holiday and Remembrance Day are **not** ESA public holidays.)

- **Public holiday pay** = regular wages earned in the 4 work weeks before
  the week with the holiday ÷ 20. Part-timers qualify; there is no minimum
  service requirement, but the "last and first rule" applies (must work the
  last scheduled shift before and first after the holiday, unless there is
  reasonable cause).
- **Working the holiday** (by agreement): either public holiday pay + premium
  pay (1.5×) for hours worked, or regular wages for the day plus a substitute
  holiday with public holiday pay.
- Note: the store is closed Sundays, which is policy, not statute — Sunday
  work refusal rights under the ESA (retail worker provisions) are mostly
  moot here but exist for retail businesses.

## Vacation

- Under 5 years of service: **2 weeks** time / **4%** vacation pay.
- 5+ years: **3 weeks** / **6%**.
- Vacation pay may be paid on each cheque for part-timers **only with written
  agreement** and shown separately on the statement.
- Vacation time is the employee's entitlement but the employer schedules it.

## Leaves of absence (job-protected, unpaid unless noted)

The ones that actually come up in a QSR:

| Leave | Entitlement | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sick leave | 3 days/yr | After 2 weeks' employment. **May not require a doctor's note for these 3 days** (ESA s. 50(6)); can require reasonable evidence like an attestation. |
| Family responsibility | 3 days/yr | Illness/urgent matter of listed relatives. |
| Bereavement | 2 days/yr | Death of listed relatives. |
| Pregnancy | up to 17 weeks | 13 weeks' service before due date. |
| Parental | up to 61/63 weeks | For birth/adoptive parents. |
| Long-term illness | up to 27 weeks | Serious medical condition, cert required (in force since mid-2025). |
| Family caregiver / medical / crime-related child death etc. | various | Look up per-case. |
| Domestic/sexual violence | 10 days + 15 weeks | First 5 days **paid**. |
| Reservist, jury, organ donor | various | Rare; look up per-case. |

Common trap: sick leave, family responsibility, and bereavement are separate
banks — an employee can use all 8 days across a year, and discipline for
ESA-protected absences is **reprisal**, which the Ministry treats seriously.

## Youth employment

- Minimum age for a restaurant (an "industrial establishment" under OHSA
  regs, which includes commercial kitchens): **15**. Front-of-house-only work
  in a purely serving capacity is still safest treated as 15+ given mixed
  duties in a QSR.
- Under-18s: student minimum wage rules above; no statutory limits on evening
  hours in Ontario (unlike some provinces), but school attendance obligations
  under the *Education Act* apply to under-18s.
- All new workers, any age: mandatory OHSA basic awareness training, WHMIS as
  applicable, and food handler certification requirements per Middlesex-London
  Health Unit (at least one certified handler on premises at all times).

## Termination entitlements (the ESA floor)

- **Notice of termination / pay in lieu** (ESA s. 57): none under 3 months of
  service; 1 week at 3 months–1 year; then 1 week per completed year to a
  **maximum of 8 weeks**. Benefits continue through the notice period.
  Vacation pay owing on termination pay.
- **Severance pay** (ESA s. 64) is *additional* and applies only if the
  employee has **5+ years** of service **and** the employer's global payroll
  is **$2.5M+**. If the payroll test is met: 1 week per year (pro-rated for
  partial years), max 26 weeks. Know your payroll number — a multi-unit or
  high-volume single unit can cross $2.5M.
- **Common law reasonable notice** is the real exposure: absent an
  enforceable termination clause, courts award far more than the ESA floor
  (rough shape: 2–6 weeks per year of service depending on age, position,
  tenure, and re-employability, capped in practice around 24 months). This is
  why the termination clause in the employment agreement matters — see the
  Waksdale note in [situation-playbooks.md](./situation-playbooks.md).
- **Temporary layoff**: do not "lay off" anyone without an express contractual
  right to do so — in Ontario a layoff without that right is constructive
  dismissal at common law even though the ESA tolerates limited layoffs.

## Other statutes in constant play

- **Human Rights Code**: protected grounds include disability, family status,
  creed, age, sex (incl. pregnancy), etc. Duty to accommodate to the point of
  undue hardship. Applies to hiring, scheduling, discipline, and termination.
- **OHSA**: workplace violence & harassment policy + program are mandatory,
  investigations of harassment complaints must be "appropriate in the
  circumstances," and both parties get written results. Annual policy review.
- **WSIA**: WSIB coverage is mandatory for restaurants. Workplace injuries
  needing health care or lost time → Form 7 within 3 business days.
  Re-employment obligations apply after work injuries (1+ year of service).
- **ESA poster/information**: provide the current "Employment Standards in
  Ontario" info to each employee within 30 days of hiring. Since Jan 1, 2026,
  employers with **25+ employees** must give new hires written information
  (legal name, contact, work location, starting wage, pay period, anticipated
  hours) before or as soon as practicable after the first day. Job postings
  now require expected compensation ranges and disclosure of AI screening use,
  and cannot require "Canadian experience"; retain postings 3 years.
- **Payroll records**: keep employment records generally 3–5 years depending
  on record type; keep them longer as litigation hygiene.
