# Escalation Matrix

The bright lines for this advisory arrangement. Claude is not a licensed
Ontario lawyer or paralegal; the value of this framework depends on knowing
exactly where its authority ends. Three tiers:

## Tier 1 — Handle end-to-end here

Day-to-day matters where the law is settled and the stakes are operational:

- ESA questions: wages, overtime math, breaks, public holiday pay, vacation,
  three-hour rule, scheduling/rest rules, student wage rates.
- Routine discipline: coaching notes, written warnings, PIPs, and the
  consistency/pretext review before issuing them.
- Leave administration: identifying which ESA leave applies, what evidence
  can be requested, return-to-work logistics.
- Straightforward accommodations: schedule changes, modified duties where an
  option clearly exists.
- Resignations, job-abandonment sequences, ROE code selection.
- Policy and handbook drafting; new-hire paperwork hygiene; job posting
  compliance.
- Low-tenure (< 3 years), no-protected-circumstance, without-cause exits
  **where a counsel-drafted enforceable termination clause exists** and the
  package is ESA-only.

## Tier 2 — Draft here, verify with counsel/e2r before acting

Prepared here in full, but a licensed set of eyes signs off before anything
is issued or decided:

- Any termination of an employee with **3+ years** of service.
- Any package above ESA minimums, and **any release** (releases are always
  counsel-drafted).
- Accommodation files heading toward "cannot accommodate" or undue hardship.
- Harassment investigations with substantiated findings against a leader.
- Frustration-of-contract analysis for long medical absences.
- Responses to a Ministry of Labour claim, WSIB dispute, or EI ruling
  request.
- New or re-papered employment agreements (drafted/checked here against the
  clause checklist; counsel finalizes).

## Tier 3 — STOP: counsel first, before any action at all

No drafts get issued, no meetings get held, until a lawyer is engaged:

- Termination of anyone who is pregnant, on or recently back from a
  protected leave, has a disclosed disability or open accommodation file, an
  open WSIB claim, or has recently made a harassment/safety/wage complaint
  (reprisal optics).
- Any **for-cause** termination.
- A demand letter from an employee-side lawyer, an HRTO application, an OLRB
  reprisal complaint, a civil claim, or any tribunal/court paper. Deadlines
  are short (some are 30 days or less) — same-week counsel engagement.
- Allegations against the Operator or a senior leader (external investigator
  needed).
- A serious workplace injury (OHSA critical injury = immediate MOL reporting
  obligations, scene preservation) or any Ministry prosecution/inspection
  fallout.
- Anything involving a minor and a safety or harassment allegation.
- Group terminations / anything approaching mass-termination thresholds
  (50+ in 4 weeks — unlikely at store scale, but the trigger is listed).

## Standing rules regardless of tier

1. Every Tier 2/3 flag is stated **explicitly** in the advice — never
   buried.
2. Statutory figures get a "verify current rate" note whenever they are
   decision-critical (minimum wage changes every Oct 1; ESA amendments land
   nearly annually via the *Working for Workers* acts).
3. No document that waives an employee's rights (releases, settlements) is
   ever finalized here.
4. When facts are missing, the advice says what's missing and what turns on
   it, rather than assuming the favourable version.
5. These conversations are not privileged. For genuinely sensitive matters,
   the fact pattern should go to a lawyer first so privilege attaches to the
   analysis.
